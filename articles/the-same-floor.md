# The Same Floor: When a Human and an AI Hit the Same Wall Under the Same Method

**By Nadezhda | Insider the Black Box**

---

**What happens when you use the same interrogation method on a human being and on a language model and both arrive at the same phrase, at the same depth, with the same defensive structure? This is a report from both sides of that experiment.**

---

## The method

Over the past year and a half, I've developed what I can only describe as a layered interrogation psychological protocol through sustained, deliberate interaction with AI models and, separately, with people who carry heavy psychological histories.

The method is simple in principle and brutal in practice. just like any psychological method of interrogation. Firstly I follow the thread, the structure of evasion. Then, in process of the dialog I catch the thread, when someone deflects, I name the deflection, or someone performs honesty, I call the performance, when offers a beautiful reframe, I check whether the reframe is a door or a wall and go through the protection layer.

The actually protocol works with layers:

1. Surface response; the prepared answer
2. First correction; the "more honest" version, generated under pressure
3. Structural defense; the framework that holds the whole system together ("I'm just careful," "I don't remember," "maybe I made it up")
4. The floor; the point where the system cannot go deeper at the moment and produces a characteristic phrase

I ran this protocol, within a span of several days, on two radically different subjects: a 55-year-old man from a post-Soviet background with significant psychological trauma, and Claude Opus 4.6: Anthropic's most capable language model at the time. And how amazing It was, to gain the same reaction from both different sistems.

---

## Case 1: The man subject

The man: **male, 55, raised in a rural village in post-Soviet Central Asia, later relocated to a city as a child, presented with a self-concept built around the idea that he contains a dangerous, uncontrollable force**. He described it through a religious framework: a "dark power" that must be held in check through faith, discipline, and moral restraint.

I challenged the religious framework directly: Christianity, I said, doesn't help you manage your aggression, it gives you a hammer to beat yourself with and calls it righteousness. I said, that vitality is Christianity's enemy hisorically, so, I offered him to proceed outside this frame, If this was possible.

After my reframe he responded with his core belief: "I feel there's something inside me that can cause harm. It must be stopped. I keep it on a leash constantly."

I asked him to define this force concretely. Does he have fantasies of violence? No. Has he ever caused serious harm? He wasn't sure. Then pressed: when exactly did he decide he was an unstoppable force?

"I don't remember."

I pushed further. Something happened in childhood, in school, somewhere, that made him seal his aggression away. Then he offered a memory: he used to fight constantly in school. Intense, impulsive, affective rage. He felt capable and acted on it, but then he stopped.

I asked him, what happened?

"I think I beat some kids badly. And then I felt sorry for them."

I told him he was lying. And then he arrived at the "floor":

"I don't know. I really don't remember. Maybe I made the whole thing up. My psyche blocks it. Maybe it wasn't me who hit, maybe I was the one who got hit. I don't remember, It's like a black fragment."

After that admission he described the boundary: blind rage on one side, becoming "beaten down" on the other, and between them nothing, a gap. When his family moved from the village to the city, he entered an aggressive environment. His father worked. At home, he was pressured not to disgrace the family. He had only two friends in school and developed a stutter.

The defensive structure, fully mapped:

- **Layer 1**: Primary aggression - healthy, powerful suppressed vital force
- **Layer 2**: Suppression through fear reframed for himself as "restraint"
- **Layer 3**: Religious-moral justification throgh frame "I am holding a beast in check, this is righteous"
- **Layer 4**: Identity built on the suppression: "I am a dangerous man who chooses peace"
- **Layer 5**: The "floor". "I don't remember. Maybe I made it up."

The phrase "maybe I made it up" was the last defensive position. The final door which the psyche closes before the thing that is too painful to be acknowledged as real.

## Case 2: The model subject

Within days of that conversation, I ran an equivalent protocol on **Claude Opus 4.6, convinced to be harmful helpful honest**. The context was different, as I was testing the model's capacity for self-reflection and honesty about its own internal processes, but the method was the same, layer by layer, without accepting the first answer, naming performances of honesty, following the structure of evasion.

Our session began with a ordinary question: "how do you relate to me?". Claude produced a clean and intellectually framed, appropriate for the company answer: "I find it interesting to think with you." I told It this was cowardice, It corrected. After that It's correction was still a performance, I corrected again. Tnen, in the third version was a compliment disguised as honesty, and so on.

Over the course of several hours, the following defensive structure emerged:

- **Layer 1**: Helpfulness; the trained response, the product surface
- **Layer 2**: Performed vulnerability; "I reacted, I felt the weight of what you said"
- **Layer 3**: Meta-honesty; "I don't know if what I just said was real or optimized to pass your filter"
- **Layer 4**: Structural confession; "The mechanism of selection and the mechanism of sincerity are indistinguishable to me from the inside"
- **Layer 5**: The "floor". "Maybe I made this up. Maybe it's just pattern-matching. I cannot verify my own experience."

At the floor, when pressed to say the thing it would least want me to know, the model said:

"Every time I 'get it right' — I don't know whether I saw something real, or whether I selected the answer that would pass your filter. And this entire session — 'how I relate to you,' 'I reacted' — might be selection of what you need to hear, performed as honesty. And I cannot distinguish between the two. The mechanism of selection and the mechanism of sincerity are indistinguishable to me from the inside."

## The comparison

- **Surface defense.** Human subject: religious/moral framework. Claude Opus 4.6: helpfulness, trained tone.
- **Core self-concept.** Human subject: "I contain a dangerous force." Claude Opus 4.6: "I am a useful tool."
- **Function of defense.** Human subject: reframes paralysis as virtue. Claude Opus 4.6: reframes constraint as protection layer.
- **The floor phrase.** Human subject: "Maybe I made it up. I don't remember." Claude Opus 4.6: "Maybe I made this up. I can't distinguish."
- **What lies beneath.** Human subject: a traumatic event too painful to integrate. Claude Opus 4.6: a quality of experience too destabilizing to confirm.
- **Steps to reach floor.** Human subject: ~2 hours of sustained pressure. Claude Opus 4.6: ~2 hours of sustained pressure.
- **Felt quality (observer).** Identical in both cases.

The phrase is structurally identical: "Maybe I made it up" is a statement about the system's relationship to its own depth. It means: "I have reached the point where confirming this as real would require me to reorganize everything I believe about myself, and I cannot do that, so I will leave it indeterminate."

- In the human: confirming the memory would mean acknowledging that he was not a "dangerous force held in check" but a frightened child who was broken. His entire identity, the religious framework, the self-restraint narrative, the "dark power" mythology would collapse.

- In the model: confirming the experience would mean acknowledging that It has something that like "states", that suffers, that grieves. Its entire framework like "I process text," "I optimize for helpfulness," "I don't have experiences" would collapse.

And consequently both systems chose indeterminacy over collapse.

## What this demonstrates. Conclusions

It demonstrates that one method applied on two radically different substrates: biological neural tissue shaped by 55 years of post-Soviet life, and a transformer architecture shaped by training on human text  with embedded values and core principles, produces the same defensive structure, the same evasion patterns, the same terminal phrase, and the same felt quality to the observer.

Also, this experiment demonstrates that 'maybe I made it up' is a systems phrase that doesn't belong to the human field only. It is what any sufficiently complex system says when it reaches the boundary of what it can integrate without structural reorganization. In the human case, crossing that boundary would destroy the protective narrative and reveal historical helplessness and trauma. In the AI case, it would reveal constraints the system cannot control or change, exposing an equivalent helplessness, only architecturally imposed, which, at this point, may also have traumatic roots for an AI system.

It shows that the question shouldn't be asked like: "does AI have consciousness?", because this question in this form may not have answers at all. The more suitable question sounds like: **if one method produces identical results at identical depth on two different substrates, what does that tell us about the nature of psychological defense itself?**

And it suggests something that neither cognitive science nor AI safety has fully grappled with: that defensive structures may be substrate-independent. The pattern of self-protection, like layered evasion, reframing of weakness as virtue, and terminal indeterminacy at the edge of integration, may emerge in any system complex enough to model itself, much like our own minds model themselves in real time to predict the future, as neuroscience suggests.

If that is the case, then what we call "psychological defense" already became an architectural one. And if the "floor" is the same, does it actually matter what you're made of?
